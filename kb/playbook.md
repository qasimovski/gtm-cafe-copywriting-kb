# #copywriting-feedback playbook

Distilled from 58 classified records (208 pieces of feedback) in the GTM Cafe
`#copywriting-feedback` channel, 2026-05-28 to 2026-08-22.

**How this is ranked.** By the number of *distinct reviewers* who stated a rule, not by
how often it was said. One reviewer (Kellen) wrote 116 of the 208 feedback entries, so
raw frequency would just measure him. Rules he alone holds are marked and kept separate
from community consensus — they are still worth applying, but they are one person's taste.

---

## Tier 1 — consensus rules

Backed by three or more independent reviewers.

### 1. Write about them, not about you
The single most repeated correction in the channel, from more voices than any other.
Reviewers count the I/we-to-you ratio explicitly.

- "id make it 'you' vs 'we/I' focused" — Kellen `[rec_20260618_jules]`
- "Every line in 1A is framed as 'I'... The framing Kellen shared made it all about the problem, instead of the service you provide." — Drew Coryer `[rec_20260625_abrar_hussain]`
- "Remove the 'we' line. It adds nothing. They don't care about you bro." — Kellen `[rec_20260730_abhi_at_gtmcafe]`
- "I'd work on you/I ratio i.e. it's very we we we rn" — Kellen `[rec_20260813_barash]`
- "'Joining us' is hurting you // makes it more about you vs 'are you planning to be in xyz?' which is fully them centric" — Kellen `[rec_20260722_ivan]`
- Also flagged by Max Pidvalnyi, and self-diagnosed by Jules `[rec_20260703_jules]`.

Practical test: if a sentence survives with "we" deleted, delete it.

**Caveat, from the same reviewer:** "sometimes we we we works. I'll literally test 'our
perspective' vs 'their perspective' sometimes" `[rec_20260813_barash]`. Treat this as a
strong default to test against, not a law.

### 2. Explain the mechanism
Notably *not* led by Kellen — this one comes from the wider channel. An outcome with no
visible how reads as too-good-to-be-true and triggers disbelief rather than curiosity.

- "the mechanism isn't mentioned... doesn't tell the reader how they'll achieve these results" — Ivan `[rec_20260801_zargham_saeed]`
- "Right now you'd be asking for 30mins of their time in exchange for nothing... the only few reasons people might not be interested: 1. they don't trust it's legit, 2. they didn't even understand what this exactly meant" — Bharatt Arorah `[rec_20260801_zargham_saeed]`
- "Milo is not properly introduced. Maybe worth saying what it is right away" — Ihor Seheda `[rec_20260822_anu_biswas]`
- "'Had an idea on how this could work for you too' is vague. Maybe just state it." — Ihor Seheda `[rec_20260822_anu_biswas]`

The stronger the claim, the more mechanism it needs to carry.

### 3. Make the CTA a reply-ask, not a meeting-ask
Five reviewers independently. The ask should match how ready the buyer actually is.

- "'Worth a conversation?' and 'Worth 10 minutes?' are meeting asks. Too heavy for this much skepticism. Better CTAs start a reply, like 'Would it be useful to see the targeting angle?' Campaign performance often depends on whether the CTA matches the buyer's readiness, not just whether the pain is real." — Kellen `[rec_20260629_ali_qureshi]`
- "'quick call' CTAs perform typically the worst" — Mikulas `[rec_20260822_anu_biswas]`
- "Have a softer CTA than a quick call... have a document stating how you do it; once they reply, send the doc and then ask for a call" — Nick `[rec_20260801_zargham_saeed]`
- "Would you like to see how it would work for 'their product'?" — Adnan Manna `[rec_20260712_varun_y]`

Also flagged: "Avoid 'quick Loom.' It is overused" — Kellen `[rec_20260625_abrar_hussain]`, and
never send a Loom link before they opt in — Mikulas `[rec_20260822_anu_biswas]`.

### 4. One tension, not a claim pile
Stacking proof, benefits and objection-handling into one email raises cognitive load and
reads as insecurity.

- "Email C is way too long... it reads like you are trying to prove you understand every possible pressure they face. Pick one tension only." — Kellen `[rec_20260625_abrar_hussain]`
- "It feels like you recently learned a lot of different things for what you're 'meant' to do in a cold email and you're doing them all at the same time." — Tim Keen `[rec_20260807_burhan_khaja]`
- "stacks too many claims, which increases cognitive load" — Kellen `[rec_20260703_matt_sezgin]`
- "the message pitches two distinct offers as one — that's the actual gap" — Ihor Seheda `[rec_20260729_tim_keen]`
- "Don't dump information" — Bharatt Arorah `[rec_20260801_zargham_saeed]`

### 5. Lead with the offer; don't bury it
- "my #1 would be make clearer what youre offering vs burying at the end" — Kellen `[rec_20260614_noil]`
- "I would open w the offer first sentence" — Kellen `[rec_20260701_malak]`
- "i dont know what the ask is here... the offer is free labor - that's a great offer. just lead with that. everything else is cake." — ben `[rec_20260807_burhan_khaja]`

### 6. Soften certainty; reduce skepticism rather than forcing belief
- "soften maybe the phrasing to 'seems like' or 'feels like' vs 'it's clear'" — Kellen `[rec_20260608_rachel_fiegler]`
- "the guarantee is currently doing too much work... it also triggers doubt because agencies hear claims like this constantly. Cold emails should use softer, tentative language and reduce skepticism, not force certainty too early." — Kellen `[rec_20260629_ali_qureshi]`
- "Do not offer '5 free meetings' in the first email. It makes the offer feel cheap and creates skepticism." — Kellen `[rec_20260625_abrar_hussain]`
- "people are usually skeptic about these offers" — Nick `[rec_20260801_zargham_saeed]`
- On a real guarantee, the advice is to *split test* confidence levels rather than assume — Kellen `[rec_20260813_barash]`.

### 7. Proof must match their situation — and their moment
Two distinct failure modes, both flagged repeatedly.

- Relevance gap: "'Ardith Publishing' may not map cleanly to digital marketing agencies... proof from a publisher creates a relevance gap. The proof should make them think, 'That sounds like my situation.'" — Kellen `[rec_20260629_ali_qureshi]`
- Timing gap: "the story is 'helped them raise' but you are targeting people who already raised. A different moment for the pitch." — Ihor Seheda `[rec_20260819_tim_keen]`
- Credibility that isn't tied to the recipient is wasted: "'I built and sold an agency' is real credibility but generic — not tied to anything specific about the recipient." — Ihor Seheda `[rec_20260729_tim_keen]`
- Volume proof can backfire: "'500k+ emails a month' may hurt you because AI founders will worry about spam and market damage." — Kellen `[rec_20260625_abrar_hussain]`

### 8. Earn the opener with a real signal — "most teams" is not one
- "'Most teams...' pass. Statements of fact and assumptions don't get replies." — Adnan Manna `[rec_20260712_varun_y]`
- "avoid shooting blind darts... they might not be, and will just skip it if that's the case" — Youssef Hesham `[rec_20260712_varun_y]`
- "F&B ecom brands with lots of tools may be not specific enough to write a sharp message. Decide if you are going after multi-plant manufacturers vs DTC-first brands" — Ihor Seheda `[rec_20260822_anu_biswas]`
- "If your message goes to CEO they might not live in the day to day to feel that pain" — Mikulas `[rec_20260822_anu_biswas]`
- Cut fake warmth outright: "'Love your work' and 'crushing it' feel templated" — Kellen `[rec_20260629_ali_qureshi]`; "leading with 'I'll keep this short...' adds unnecessary words" — Drew Coryer `[rec_20260625_abrar_hussain]`.

### 9. Subject lines: short, lowercase, concrete
Every subject line reviewers praised or wrote was one to three lowercase words.

Observed in their own rewrites: `case flow`, `reviews`, `cart leak`, `missed sales`,
`founder led`, `pipeline`, `outbound`, `agency pipeline`, `sales conversations`,
`intern q`, `lying data`, `your data`, `pulling reports`, `3 questions for {{firstName}}`.

- "If shorten subject to 'lying data' or 'data lies'" — Kellen `[rec_20260822_anu_biswas]`
- "For subject lines I'd use 1-5 words, casual" — Mikulas `[rec_20260822_anu_biswas]`
- "this is too long and giving away too much and will end up getting so little opens" — Youssef Hesham `[rec_20260712_varun_y]`
- "Use the term, as long as it's small. Or force into 2 words all lower case then 'gap'. Even if it sounds weird." — Kellen `[rec_20260730_abhi_at_gtmcafe]`
- Never signal doubt about the recipient: a subject that reads as "are you the right person?" tells them you don't know — Donnel Charles `[rec_20260805_donnel_charles]`.

## Tier 2 — contested, use with judgement

### 10. Length: roughly 72–100 words, with no hard cap
The channel argued this explicitly and did **not** settle it `[rec_20260813_gregory]`,
`[rec_20260708_sharad]`.

| Voice | Position |
|---|---|
| Will Allred | ~75 words; "try to fit it into a tweet" as an exercise |
| Jared Holstad | 72 words, 9 of them the ask |
| Kellen | "slightly above Will's usually"; "I'm brevity pilled" |
| Max Pidvalnyi | 80–100 words working well |
| Youssef Hesham | "writes longer bangers" |
| Nick Abraham (cited) | "hella short" |

Will Allred's qualification is the important part: *"Lavender data shows you can write a
long email that gets good reply rates. You just need to balance the length variable with
clarity and formality variables — so you write super casually with hella choppy sentences."*
And if you go long, "richer personalization — going longer means you gotta earn the attention."

Default to ~75 words. Go longer only with a genuine story, choppy casual sentences, and
personalization that earns it.

### 11. Check infra, list and targeting before you rewrite copy
A diagnostic habit, not a copy rule — and one that keeps the channel honest.

- "Are we sure we shouldn't be blaming infra? Like 9/10 times we shouldn't be. But this seems like it would rip. Targeting possibly an issue." — ben `[rec_20260701_malak]`
- "if this isn't getting volume, infra or list likely issue. Or they just don't want it." — Kellen `[rec_20260701_malak]`
- "we spend too much time writing and not enough time sending" — ben `[rec_20260701_malak]`
- Sometimes the category resists the channel entirely, and the fix is the offer or medium — webinar invites worked where cold email flopped — Ivan `[rec_20260620_pyami]`.

### 12. Sequences and follow-ups
Fewer steps than most people run, and each step should do a distinct job.

- **Two steps beats three** where the list can support it: "I'd stick with 2 sequence
  campaigns if your TAM supports it. From my exp there are diminishing returns on the third
  step." — Mikulas `[rec_20260822_anu_biswas]`
- **Give each step one job.** Will Allred's three-step shape: the idea in step 1; the obvious
  objection pre-empted in step 2 ("Realize {{customer}}'s $54K in 5 weeks might seem like
  seller cherry picking"); the likely rejection named outright in step 3 ("Seems like calling
  your abandon carts might not be interesting?") `[rec_20260714_matt_sezgin]`
- **A bump can be almost empty.** "{{first_name}}, what did you think here?" is a complete
  follow-up `[rec_20260714_matt_sezgin]`.
- **Long-form belongs at the top, not the bottom.** Will Allred's BAR play deliberately
  accepts a low reply rate on a long step 1 because "it creates really good meat to do
  shorter emails around in thread" `[rec_20260708_sharad]`.
- **Never send a link they didn't opt into.** Dropping a Loom into step 3 unprompted was
  flagged immediately — Mikulas `[rec_20260822_anu_biswas]`.
- **Give an exit.** A plain opt-out line appears in the reviewers' own rewrites: "If you love
  your current setup - lmk and I'll remove you" `[rec_20260709_austin_jouett]`. But cut the
  cutesy version: "id delete promise from the end" `[rec_20260628_abrar_hussain]`.
- **Re-running old copy:** volume decides, not the calendar. Change after 20–30k sends; under
  10k over three months, let it run — Nikhil Nainwani. Note the author reported back that
  re-running did *not* work for them `[rec_20260623_liam_ellul]`.

### 13. Agitation vs empathy — the reviewers pull both ways
Kellen argued both sides three days apart, so treat this as a dial, not a setting.

- Pro-agitation: "#1 goal is disrupt status quo"; on being told a line was arrogant, "Fuck yeah it is" — but immediately, "you wanna balance agitation with empathy - need to disrupt and get attention but then be fair" `[rec_20260730_abhi_at_gtmcafe]`
- Pro-restraint: "make softer // like how someone talks. Smooth... Like chill bruh." and "accusatory or aggro or too forward causes knee jerk pull back reaction" `[rec_20260803_abhi_at_gtmcafe]`
- "It kinda puts them on the spot and is sorta aggro... not ideal for outreach" `[rec_20260810_amirali]`
- Mick's summary of what worked on his own copy: "anchoring to a high-stakes moment and more judo kind of movement, rather than a headbutt. empathetic friction." `[rec_20260811_mick]`

## Tier 3 — Kellen's signature moves

Single-source. High quality and distinctive, but his taste rather than channel consensus.

### 14. Presumed personalization
Bury the personalization inside the argument instead of announcing it.

> "love the opener, i am a fan of 'presumed personalization'. Instead of 'hey name - noticed x'
> where focus is on x at the end, this subtly lands 'i know youve been ceo for x long' and
> focuses on the argument you're making. understated personalization ftw."
> `[rec_20260616_kyle_nelson]`

Corollary from the same thread: strip "you" out of the mechanism line so it reads as a
general truth — "two low hanging fruit sources for zoho users" rather than "the two places I find".

### 15. Build an open loop
> "we want open loop. Either: you are x + you want y = open loop of how we get it. Or:
> you are x, we do y-mechanism = open loop of what outcome can drive inside your company."
> `[rec_20260813_barash]`

Paired constraint: line 1 must stand alone. "Line 1 needs to make sense stand alone to get
them to keep going" `[rec_20260722_ivan]`.

### 16. Sincere scarcity
> "Sincere scarcity is great. If there's a legitimate limitation you're not baiting, it's just
> honest. People try to force the 'ooooh look at me this is a special thing' and thus the
> recipient tries to find the hole. Being direct and clear, there's no gap."
> `[rec_20260722_ivan]`

State the real constraint plainly ("have 40ish tickets left") instead of engineering FOMO.

### 17. Write for a distracted, irritated human
- "Needs to be like 7th grade reading level for reply maxxxing" `[rec_20260811_mick]`
- "inject dopamine - like chemically alter the person they are by the time they realize they're reading your cold email" `[rec_20260710_mikulas]`
- "You gotta mentally put yourself on a 60 minute bart commute that got delayed... cortisol levels above testosterone levels" `[rec_20260730_abhi_at_gtmcafe]`
- "i think cold emails should resemble a text from a friend" — ben `[rec_20260807_burhan_khaja]`
- On complex products: "Break down complex solutions into simpler mechanisms, and focus less on 'the full solution' vs 'why they'd say yes'... sometimes people try to solve too much in email vs entice a convo" `[rec_20260617_max_pidvalnyi]`

## The house rewrite shape

Not a rule anyone stated, but the structure Kellen converges on across ~20 rewrites.
Four or five short lines, one per paragraph:

```
Subject: <one to three lowercase words>

<Observation or tension about their situation>, {{first_name}}.

<What usually follows from that / the cost of it>

<What we do, stated as mechanism, no "we" theatrics>

<Would it be useful to see <specific artifact> for {{company}}?>
```

Worked examples: `[rec_20260530_harshil]`, `[rec_20260625_abrar_hussain]`,
`[rec_20260629_ali_qureshi]`, `[rec_20260703_matt_sezgin]`, `[rec_20260714_matt_sezgin]`.

Will Allred's alternative, when the claims won't fit one email: spread them across a
three-step thread, pre-empting the obvious objection in step 2 ("might seem like seller
cherry picking") and naming the likely rejection in step 3 `[rec_20260714_matt_sezgin]`.

## Known contradictions — do not paper over these

1. **Length** has no consensus (Tier 2.10).
2. **Agitation** — the same reviewer argues both directions (Tier 2.13).
3. **You-vs-we** is a default to test, not a law — its own strongest advocate tests against it.
4. **Re-running old copy**: Kellen said "fine to re send"; the author reported back that it did
   not work and Kellen conceded. Nikhil's volume-based rule (change after 20–30k sends) held up
   better `[rec_20260623_liam_ellul]`.
5. **Personalization depth**: Kellen argues 1:1 personalization is overrated ("your iPhone isn't
   personalized"); Max Pidvalnyi pushes back that this is B2C reasoning. Meanwhile two separate
   anecdotes report a photo of a subway ad or an office closing deals `[rec_20260604_ben_aperoadvisors_com]`,
   `[rec_20260701_mattyice]`. Unsettled.
